# Product Charter

## Product

**Engineering Control Tower** — an executive operating system for engineering portfolio health and intervention intelligence.

## Problem

Engineering leaders receive abundant status but limited decision intelligence. Delivery, quality, security, reliability, and capacity evidence lives in separate tools and forums. Portfolio reviews often describe individual teams while missing the shared constraint that determines the outcome.

## Product promise

Make the engineering system legible enough for leaders to find the constraint, choose the intervention, name the owner, and verify that the signal changed.

## Primary users

- CTO and engineering executive staff
- Senior/principal technology program and portfolio leaders
- Engineering and platform directors
- Product, security, reliability, and architecture leaders
- Transformation and operating-model leaders

## Core use cases

1. Run a weekly executive engineering portfolio review.
2. Compare program health without flattening team context.
3. Identify shared-service, environment, specialist, and decision-latency constraints.
4. Explain why a score changed and which rule was activated.
5. Prioritize a small decision queue by intervention value.
6. Produce a concise, evidence-grounded executive narrative.

## Non-goals for V1

- Replacing Jira, GitHub, CI/CD, incident, security, or observability platforms
- Predicting individual performance
- Automating executive decisions
- Connecting to employer systems or using real enterprise data

## Success measures

- A leader can identify the top portfolio constraint in under 60 seconds.
- Every elevated signal exposes its evidence, driver, action, owner, and verification date.
- Two reviewers can reproduce the same score from the same inputs.
- The public application functions with no credentials or external services.

## Product principles

- Outcomes before activity
- System constraints before local symptoms
- Explainability before sophistication
- Decisions before dashboards
- Evidence before narrative
- Teams are contexts, not rankings
